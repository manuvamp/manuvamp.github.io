Flashcards from video: what survives a month

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Video notes have a failure mode: they read well the day you write them and never again. A month later they are a wall of prose you must re-read in full to recover a single idea. Flashcards fix the format problem, but only if you follow one rule while making them.

The one-claim rule

A card must ask about exactly one claim, in a question you could hear and answer out loud. "What does ORANO do with tasks?" works. "ORANO features" does not. The rule forces you to decide what the video actually taught, which is the thinking work most notes skip. When a video is converted this way, you discover most hours contain two or three keepable claims and a lot of connective tissue.

The review schedule does the remembering

Cards without a schedule are just notes with extra steps. Spaced review — one day, one week, one month — is what moves a claim from "heard" to "known." ORANO generates the cards from any saved video and schedules them automatically, so the discipline is built in rather than hoped for.

The payoff

A month later you will have forgotten the video and retained the claims. That asymmetry is the entire point: the content was the medium, the cards are the asset.
