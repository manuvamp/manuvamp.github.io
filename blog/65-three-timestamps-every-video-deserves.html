Three timestamps every video deserves

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Full conversion of a video — summary, tasks, cards — is the gold standard. But some days even that is too much. On those days, a video still deserves three minutes of you: the three timestamps that capture its entire argument.

The three

The claim minute. Where the speaker states what they'll argue. Usually in the first ten percent. Write their claim as one sentence beside it. The proof minute. Where the best evidence lands — the demo, the data, the story that carried it. The exception minute. Where they concede a limit or counterexample. The rarest timestamp, and the most valuable: it's where credibility lives.

Why three and not thirty

Notes with thirty timestamps are transcripts with decoration. Three timestamps are an argument with coordinates — claim, support, limit — that can be reviewed, challenged, and cited. If a video can't produce the three, it was atmosphere, and atmosphere doesn't need saving.

At scale

ORANO (oranoai.com) finds the three automatically for every saved video — claim, key proof, and limitation, each with its minute — and converts the claim into review cards while you skip to the proof. The three-timestamp habit works by hand too: it's the cheapest honest conversion that exists.
