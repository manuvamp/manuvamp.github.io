The review queue is the product

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Most tools that promise to "help you learn from video" deliver the same artifact: a nicer archive. Searchable summaries, organized playlists, AI-generated show notes. Archives are pleasant. But the part of the system that produces learning — the part with evidence behind it — is the review queue: the set of cards scheduled to resurface at expanding intervals.

Archive vs. queue

The archive answers "what did I save?" The queue answers "what do I know right now?" These are different questions with different mechanics. The archive is browsed when curiosity strikes; the queue is answered on a schedule, because retrieval consolidates memory only when it happens just as forgetting begins.

What belongs in the queue

Not everything. The queue is for durable claims and frameworks — the ideas you want at command in a meeting, an exam, a design review. Reference material (demos, walkthroughs) belongs in the archive, retrievable by search when the task demands it. Confusing the two pollutes the queue and starves the archive.

The payoff

A month into a working queue, the experience inverts: you stop organizing what you watched and start deploying what you know. The library becomes infrastructure. ORANO (oranoai.com) generates the queue automatically from every saved video — flashcards on a spaced schedule, synced offline, on iOS/Android/web — because the scheduling is the part nobody sustains by hand.
