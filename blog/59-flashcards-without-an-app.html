Flashcards are not school supplies

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Somewhere between graduation and the first job, professionals abandon the single most evidence-backed learning technique they ever used: the flashcard. We keep the highlighters and lose the retrieval schedule, then wonder why a book we read in March needs re-reading in October.

What professionals actually need to retain

Not trivia — decisions and their reasons, frameworks and their boundaries, vocabulary and its precision. A startup founder benefits from instantly recalling "why we rejected usage-based pricing." An engineer benefits from "what the cap theorem actually guarantees." These are cards, whether or not we call them that.

The two objections, answered

"Cards feel juvenile." The format is irrelevant; the retrieval schedule is the mechanism. A card can live in your notes app, phrased as a question you'd genuinely ask. "Making cards takes time." Yes — about ten minutes per lecture or meeting, which is why conversion should be automated. ORANO generates the cards from any saved video or recording and runs the schedule; the professional supplies only the ninety-second answers.

The payoff

Experts aren't people who read more; they're people who retained more of what they read. The queue is how retention stops being luck.
