From webinar recording to workshop recap

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

A team attends a two-hour webinar. One person took notes; the notes are in their private doc; the recording is in a drive. In a month, the collective memory is "something about pricing tiers?" Here's what the recap page should contain instead — a worked example of the one-page brief standard.

The four sections, filled in

Claim: "Packaging beats discounting for B2B retention" @ 06:12. Takeaways: five points, each with the evidence the presenter offered — sample sizes, counterexamples, the one caveat they volunteered at 1:41:00. Follow-ups: "map our tiers against their matrix" (owner: pricing lead), "audit last quarter's discounts" (owner: finance). Cards: two Q&As for the retention concept the webinar hinged on.

The timestamps are the team's time machine

When someone challenges a takeaway, the recap links to the exact minute. Discussion ends where the evidence starts. This single property — checkability — is why the recap page gets cited in decisions while the recording itself is never opened.

The payoff

Two hours of attendance becomes one reusable page. Multiply by every webinar the team attends and the math stops being embarrassing. ORANO (oranoai.com) generates the recap automatically from the recording; the standard is the part worth adopting even by hand.
