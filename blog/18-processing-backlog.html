Processing the backlog: four hundred saves, one evening

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Everyone who saves things has a number. Mine was around four hundred — videos, articles, repos, accumulated over years, each saved by a person who sincerely meant it. The backlog feels like a monument to failure. It is not. It is unsorted raw material, and unlike New Year's resolutions, raw material keeps.

The one-evening method

  • Sort by oldest, not newest. Recent saves still have intent in them. Old ones need a verdict.
  • Thirty seconds per item, no exceptions. Longer means you are watching the video, and watching is how the backlog got built.
  • Three outcomes only. Process it now (it becomes a project, a roadmap, or extracted key points), keep it as reference (key points extracted, source attached), or delete it. There is no fourth state called "decide later".

Why it finally works in 2026

The reason backlog processing failed for a decade is that "process" meant manual extraction — pausing, transcribing, typing steps. That cost twenty minutes an item, which is why four hundred items felt like four hundred evenings. When ORANO does the understanding — transcript, key points, action classification, task lists with estimates — thirty seconds per item is enough, because the decision is all that's left for you. Four hundred items, one evening, and a library where every survivor has a reason to exist.

Maintenance is cheaper than the rescue: the input diet keeps new saves honest, and a weekly audit keeps the library sharp. ORANO is on iOS and Android.
