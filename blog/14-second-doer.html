The second brain is full. Build a second do-er.

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

A decade of personal knowledge management taught people one skill with great precision: how to store things they will never open. The second brain fills, the folders multiply, the tagging system gets a tagging system. And in the real world, nothing moves — because storage was never the missing organ.

What a do-er does differently

A do-er takes the same input — a saved video, a lecture, a recipe Reel, a repo — and its first question is not "where do I file this?" but "what is the next physical action this implies?" That reframe kills most saves on arrival: a surprising share of what you capture has no action in it at all, and pretending otherwise is how graveyards form.

  • Actionable content becomes a plan: ordered steps, time estimates, a first task you can start tonight.
  • Learning content becomes a curriculum: what to learn, in what order, with the source attached.
  • Reference stays reference — deliberately kept, key points already extracted, not re-watchable guilt.

Memory still matters — for your tools, not just you

The quiet shift in 2026 is that your notes now have readers other than you. Your AI assistant is only as useful as the context it can see. A second brain that an agent cannot read is a diary; one it can is an operating system. That is why ORANO treats the loop as one pipeline — capture, understand, plan, execute, remember — and then exposes the useful part of that memory to your own AI tools through a personal, read-only MCP server, so your agent plans Monday with what you saved on Saturday.
