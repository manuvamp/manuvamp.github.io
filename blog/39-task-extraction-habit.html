The task extraction habit

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Watch any good tutorial or conference talk and count the imperatives. Set this up. Try that pattern. Read this book. Run this experiment. A solid hour of content typically contains five to ten disguised tasks — and by default all of them evaporate the moment the video ends.

Extraction at save time, not later

The extraction has to happen when you save, not when you "get around to it," because the second date never arrives. The habit: for every saved video, capture the imperative sentences as tasks with a verb and a scope. "Try the new embedding model on our search corpus" is a task. "Video on embeddings" is a bookmark wearing a costume.

Not every video deserves it

The discipline doubles as a filter. If a video yields zero tasks and zero flashcards, it was entertainment — which is fine, as long as it stops occupying your learning queue. ORANO automates the extraction — saved videos arrive in your library with their implied tasks already pulled out and listed — but the two-bucket judgment (learning vs. entertainment) is yours, and it is the valuable part.

The payoff

Your task list starts collecting the actionable fraction of everything you watch, and your watch queue shrinks to what genuinely deserves an hour of your eyes and ears.
