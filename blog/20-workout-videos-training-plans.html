Workout Reels into training plans: the fitness save that actually happens

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Fitness content is the most-saved, least-used category on every platform. The mechanics are brutal: a 45-second Reel demonstrates a perfect superset, you save it with genuine intent, and then — nothing. Not because you lost motivation, but because by Thursday you cannot remember which Reel had the dumbbell complex, what the rep scheme was, or how it fits with the two other saves from the same week.

The conversion that changes everything

A saved workout becomes a usable training session when four things are extracted:

  • The movements, in order, with sets and reps as actually instructed (spoken and on-screen text often differ — both matter).
  • The equipment list, so you can train without scrubbing the video at the gym.
  • The estimated duration, so it fits the gap in your calendar.
  • The progression context — is this a finisher, a main lift day, or a weekly program?

What this looks like in practice

Share the Reel into ORANO and the extraction happens automatically: the speech is transcribed, on-screen text is read separately, and the save arrives as a structured project — movements ordered, equipment listed, duration estimated. A week of saved Reels becomes a reviewable set of sessions; you pick one, see the plan, and train. When the plan is the tap that follows the save, the save stops being a wish.

The same pipeline handles meal-prep videos (ingredients + steps + cost), running-form breakdowns, and mobility routines — one library, every save actionable. ORANO is free on iOS and Android.
