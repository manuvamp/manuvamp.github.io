A review cadence for team knowledge

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Teams don't forget because they lack notes. They forget because nothing resurfaces the notes at the moment they'd matter. The fix is not more documentation — it's a review cadence: fixed checkpoints where past knowledge is re-presented to the people who need it.

The three checkpoints

Project opens: review every brief related to the project's domain — past decisions, constraints, customer quotes. Ten minutes, and the project avoids re-learning what the company already paid to learn. Meeting opens: ninety seconds of decision cards from prior meetings. Re-litigation dies here. Month closes: one theme review — all briefs tagged with the month's focus topic, skimmed for anything that changed. Twenty minutes.

Why cadence beats search

Search retrieves only what someone thinks to look for. Cadence resurfaces what they didn't know to ask about — the constraint everyone forgot, the customer quote that pre-answers the new feature debate. Search is a tool; cadence is a system.

Making it survive

The cadence dies when generating the review material is manual. Automate the generation — at ORANO, every saved meeting and video already produces its brief and cards, so the checkpoints are review-only — and keep the forgiveness rule: a skipped checkpoint is skipped, not rescheduled into guilt.
