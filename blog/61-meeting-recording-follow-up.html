The 24-hour follow-up rule for recorded meetings

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

A meeting recap loses half its value every day it waits. Sent within 24 hours, it steers the work. Sent within 24 hours with timestamps, it also settles disputes. Sent never — the default — it contributes nothing. The rule: every recorded meeting gets a recap within a day.

The recap template

Decisions (each one sentence, with the recording minute). Open questions (each with an owner). Actions (verb-first, with dates). Nothing else. A recap that requires scrolling is a recap nobody reads — and the recording link goes at the bottom, because the recap is the product and the recording is the source.

Where the time goes

Writing the recap by hand takes fifteen minutes and is the reason the rule fails in practice. Automating the draft takes it to two: ORANO converts the saved recording into the decision list, open questions, and owner-assigned actions directly — the human pass is correction, not creation. The 24-hour rule survives because the cost collapsed.

The payoff

Teammates who missed the meeting read instead of watching. Decisions get challenged early, while changing them is cheap. And the "what did we say about X?" question gets answered by a search instead of a meeting.
