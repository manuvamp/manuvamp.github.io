Podcast show notes into a content engine

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every podcaster sits on an archive that publishes once and then dies: the episode drops, the show notes live for a week, and a hundred hours of recorded thinking become unfindable. The audience asked for "an episode about X" last month and got a shrug. The fix is treating the archive as raw material for a content engine, not as a feed.

The brief per episode

Each recording converts to a standard brief: the episode's central claim, three to five key points with timestamps, every resource mentioned as a link, and the best two exchange quotes verbatim. This brief is publishable as show notes, sliceable into newsletter sections, and quotable in future episodes. ORANO generates it from the audio automatically — the host records, the brief exists before promotion starts.

The compounding layers

Once briefs exist: search engines index claims instead of titles; listeners cite minutes instead of vibes; clips get cut from the timestamps that proved quotable; and a "start here" page can route new listeners by topic instead of by episode number. The archive becomes a library with a librarian.

The payoff

Back-catalog episodes start earning downloads months after release, guests share briefs that flatter them accurately, and the next pitch to sponsors includes a search-friendly library instead of a download graph.
