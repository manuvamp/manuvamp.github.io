Five video habits of people who actually learn from them

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Two people can watch the same lecture. One can teach it a week later; the other remembers it was "pretty good." The difference is never intelligence or memory — it's five small habits, none of which take more than a minute.

The habits

1. State the why before saving. One sentence: what this video will teach. Unwritable? It's entertainment — enjoy it, skip the guilt.

2. Convert at save-time. The summary, the tasks, the cards — generated while the content is fresh, when conversion is cheapest. Later-conversion is where libraries die.

3. Timestamp everything. Notes with minutes are citations — checkable in fifteen seconds. Notes without are rumors.

4. Review on a calendar, not a mood. Cards resurface at day 3, 10, 30. Retrieval on schedule is what moves ideas from recognized to known.

5. Retire what's mastered. Cards answered cleanly three times leave the deck. The deck should feel hard; your head should feel full.

The only metric

Not hours watched, not videos saved — claims retrievable a month later. Everything above serves that number. ORANO (oranoai.com) automates habits 2-4 for any saved video on iOS, Android, and web; habits 1 and 5 are yours.
