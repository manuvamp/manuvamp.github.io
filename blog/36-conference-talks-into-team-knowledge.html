Conference talks into team knowledge

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every conference produces the same artifact: a folder of recordings that everyone means to watch and nobody does. The talks contain real signal — vendor-agnostic architecture lessons, post-mortems, tool demos — but an hour of video is the least skimmable format in existence.

Summary first, watching optional

Invert the default. Each talk should produce a one-page brief before anyone decides to watch: the speaker's central claim, the two strongest supporting points, and what the speaker would do differently. With that brief, most people can skip the talk entirely and still benefit. The ones who need depth know exactly which segment to watch, because the summary carries timestamps. ORANO generates these briefs from the recording automatically.

Claims become tasks or trash

A conference talk that ends without a change to your roadmap was entertainment. During processing, force every "you should adopt X" into one of two buckets: a task with an owner, or an explicit "not for us, because...". The second bucket matters more than teams admit — it converts vague anxiety into a documented decision.

The payoff

The conference folder stops being a guilt pile and becomes an indexed knowledge base your team can actually query — and next year's ticket gets easier to justify.
