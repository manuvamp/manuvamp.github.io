The one-page brief standard

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Formats are leverage. When a team agrees on one format for recorded knowledge — one page per video, four sections, timestamps on every claim — everything downstream gets easier: search, onboarding, decisions, even tool choices. The recording becomes a source; the page becomes the product.

The four sections

The claim. One sentence with a timestamp: what the video argues. The takeaways. Up to five points, each with its evidence. The follow-ups. Every implied action as a task with an owner. The cards. Two or three question/answer pairs for the concepts worth retaining.

Why one page, why four sections

One page is the largest unit a person will read without being asked. Four sections is the smallest set that covers decide (claim), understand (takeaways), act (follow-ups), and retain (cards). Anything more is hoarding; anything less loses one of the four jobs.

Adopting it

Start with new recordings only — don't backfill. Write the first five by hand to learn the format, then let a tool carry the load. ORANO (oranoai.com) produces exactly this page from any saved video or audio automatically; the standard is what matters, the automation is a convenience.
