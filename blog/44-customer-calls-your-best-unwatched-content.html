Customer calls: your best unwatched content

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Companies record customer calls for compliance and then never use them. That's backwards: an hour with a real customer contains more product truth than a quarter of internal meetings. The recording exists. What's missing is a way to consume fifty hours of calls as a team.

From call recording to brief

The unit of value is not the call; it's the one-page brief the call produces: who the customer is, what they tried before yours, the exact words they used for the problem (verbatim quotes are gold — marketing will thank you), and the feature requests separated from the underlying need. Once calls are briefs, patterns across fifty customers become visible in an afternoon of reading.

Requests vs. needs

The separation matters because customers are reliable about problems and unreliable about solutions. "Add a kanban view" is a request; "I can't see team workload at a glance" is the need. A summary that keeps both, clearly labeled, lets the team decide — and the timestamped source settles any argument about what was actually said.

The pipeline

Record → generate the brief with key points and timestamps → extract tasks (follow-ups with the customer, feature considerations) → file it where the team searches. ORANO runs this pipeline for any saved video or audio, but even a manual version beats the compliance-only folder your calls live in today.
