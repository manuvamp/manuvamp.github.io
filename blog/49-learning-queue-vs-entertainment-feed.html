Your learning queue vs. your entertainment feed

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

There are two kinds of videos in every saved queue, and the trouble starts when they share a folder. Entertainment — a great video essay, a cooking montage, a highlight reel — is genuinely valuable. Learning content — the tutorial, the lecture, the teardown — demands conversion. Mixing them means the entertainment generates guilt it didn't earn, and the learning generates no action it deserved.

The one-second test

At save time, ask: will I make flashcards from this, or watch it once? Flashcards means learning — it enters a queue with summaries, extracted tasks, and a review schedule. Watch-once means entertainment — no obligations, no guilt, delete after viewing. One tag, one second, and both categories get what they need.

Why entertainment needs a home too

This isn't a war on fun. A video essay you loved is reference material for taste, humor, pacing — but it shouldn't sit in a queue labeled "learn Rust." When the categories are honest, the learning queue shrinks to what you'll actually process, and the fun stops feeling like procrastination.

The payoff

ORANO (oranoai.com) is built for the first bucket — it converts learning saves into notes, tasks, and flashcards automatically. But the tag alone, in any system, fixes the biggest problem: a queue where every item has a stated reason to exist.
