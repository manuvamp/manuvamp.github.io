The second-brain audit

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Quarterly, I audit my capture systems the way an accountant audits books. Not to organize — to discover the gap between what I captured and what I can actually use. The audit takes one hour and consistently finds that two-thirds of everything I saved has become inert.

The four questions

What did I save this quarter? Raw count, by app. Most people underestimate by half — saves scatter across browsers, phones, and read-later apps. What can I retrieve right now? Pick ten random saves and state their content without opening them. Honest score: usually two or three. What changed because I saved it? The hardest question. Saves that produced no action and no memory were entertainment wearing a learning costume. What's the debt? The count of saves that would take longer to process now than the content is worth. That's deletion inventory.

The corrective actions

The audit reliably produces the same three fixes: convert at save-time (not someday), attach every save to a review schedule, and merge capture apps until there's one. For video specifically, ORANO handles the conversion step — saved videos arrive as timestamped summaries with tasks and cards — so the audit finds a working library instead of a landfill.

The payoff

A second brain you never audit is a diary with ambitions. One hour a quarter keeps it honest.
