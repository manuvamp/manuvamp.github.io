Your video library is an onboarding asset

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Ask a new hire what onboarding actually consists of and the answer is usually variations on "people explained things to me, some of it twice." Meanwhile the company owns hundreds of hours of recordings — demos, architecture walkthroughs, customer calls — that contain most of that knowledge already. The library exists. The bridge from library to new hire does not.

Why the recordings don't help

An unprocessed recording is a cost to a newcomer, not an asset: they don't know what's in it, which twenty minutes matter, or what has changed since it was recorded. Watching archives in order is the slowest possible way to learn a company.

The conversion that changes it

Process each relevant recording into a brief: the claim, the context, five key points with timestamps, and the follow-ups it generated. Group the briefs by topic instead of by date — "how we handle pricing objections" becomes one page citing six calls at specific minutes. A new hire reads structured pages for two days and arrives at their first project already oriented. ORANO generates those briefs automatically from any saved video or audio, which is why our own onboarding now starts with a reading list, not a meeting marathon.

The payoff

Senior people stop re-explaining, the library finally earns its storage, and the company's knowledge survives the people who first explained it.
