Cite the minute

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

"I remember it differently" is the most expensive sentence in team communication. It converts a work conversation into a memory contest, and memory contests have no winners — only meetings that re-open old ground. There's a cure, and it costs one habit: cite the minute.

The habit

Any reference to a recorded meeting, call, or talk carries its minute-mark. "The renewal decision — see 23:40." "The customer said it exactly at 14:02." The habit is trivial during note-taking and transformative during disagreement.

What minute-citations change

Arguments shorten. A challenged claim gets checked in fifteen seconds; the debate moves from memory to substance. Newcomers onboard faster. They read decisions with pointers to their evidence instead of watching archaeology footage. Trust moves to the system. When the record is reliably checkable, people stop hedging in meetings — they know the minutes will hold.

The cost

Note-taking gains one field: the minute. That's it. And when volume makes manual minute-marking unsustainable, that's precisely the field automation handles best — ORANO (oranoai.com) attaches timestamps to every summary point, task, and card it generates from saved recordings.
