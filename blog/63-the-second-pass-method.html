The second pass method

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

The advice "watch it twice" is backwards. Full re-watching reviews what you already understood at the same pace as what you missed. The second pass method spends a fraction of that time — because it only touches the gaps.

Pass one: watch for the skeleton

Normal speed or 1.5x. Output: the video's central claim in one sentence, and a flag on any segment you couldn't summarize. Most videos yield a complete skeleton in a single pass — and need no second pass at all.

The conversion

Between passes, convert: key points with timestamps, implied tasks, cards for durable concepts. This is where learning happens — compression forces comprehension. ORANO does this conversion automatically at save-time, so the skeleton exists before you'd ever think about re-watching.

Pass two: gaps only

If any segment was flagged, re-watch just those minutes — now with a specific question in mind. Targeted replay of ten minutes beats sequential replay of an hour, because you arrive knowing what you're looking for.

The payoff

Total time: one full watch plus minutes of targeted review — for retention that sequential re-watching never produced. The video becomes a source you can navigate, not a task you avoid.
