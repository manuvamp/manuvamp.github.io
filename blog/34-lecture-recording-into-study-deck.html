Lecture recordings into a study deck

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

The default behavior after a lecture is to keep the recording "just in case" and never open it again. Rewatching is the study method of last resort — it costs the full hour and reviews everything at the same speed, including the parts you already know.

Compression before review

A recording should become three artifacts before you ever rewatch it: a one-page summary with timestamps, the follow-up actions the lecturer implied, and a deck of question/answer cards for the durable concepts. Once those exist, the recording is reference material, not homework. Drop the link into ORANO and the three artifacts are generated for you; the deck lands in your review queue automatically.

Spaced review beats cramming

The deck is the part most students skip and the part that pays. Ten minutes of cards three days after the lecture outperforms a full rewatch the night before the exam, because retrieval — not exposure — is what builds memory. ORANO schedules the cards so the timing is not another decision to make.

The payoff

Each lecture costs one pass of attention instead of three. Your recordings turn from a guilt folder into a searchable index, and exam week becomes review instead of panic.
