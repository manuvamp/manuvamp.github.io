From native content to study plan: language learning without the spreadsheet

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every language learner eventually gets the same advice: stop drilling apps, go consume native content. Everyone who takes it discovers the same problem — native content is unstructured chaos. A cooking Reel in Spanish teaches you six words and leaves no trace. Two hundred saves later you have a folder called "spanish stuff" and a vague feeling of failure.

What a save should become

A saved video in your target language is raw curriculum if someone does the structuring work:

  • The vocabulary actually used, not the textbook list — with the moment in the video it came from.
  • The grammar pattern the native speaker used, at real speed, in real context.
  • A sensible order: what to study before you rewatch.

The reason nobody does this manually is obvious: it is hours of pausing and transcribing per hour of content. It only works if the structuring is automatic.

The pipeline

In ORANO, the flow is: share the Reel or video → the app transcribes the speech and reads the visual context → you get a summary, the key language points, and for learning-type saves, an ordered study roadmap with steps and time estimates. Ten saved videos become a week of lessons instead of a digital pile. The library stays searchable, so "that one video that explained the subjunctive" takes seconds to find again.

It works the same for lectures, tutorials, and how-to content in any field — native content in your discipline, not just your language. iOS · Android.
