The 30-day purge

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every saved-content system needs one deletion rule, applied monthly: anything unconverted after 30 days gets deleted. Not archived — deleted. The rule sounds harsh until you run it once and discover what it protects.

Why 30 days

Conversion cost doubles roughly every month an item waits. Today, you remember why you saved it; the summary writes itself in five minutes. In 30 days it takes twenty. At 60, you're reconstructing context that no longer exists — and the only honest settlement is deletion. The 30-day line catches items while they're still cheap to process.

The purge, in four passes

Pass 1 — the obviously dead. Anything you can't explain saving. Fastest deletes you'll ever make. Pass 2 — the expired. Trigger conditions that fired and passed, needs that dissolved. Pass 3 — the convertibles. What remains gets summarized, tasked, and carded — in bulk, ideally. Pass 4 — the verdicts. Learned, reference, or entertainment, honestly labeled for the first time.

Why deletion is first-class

Systems that treat deletion as failure train you to hoard. Systems that treat it as maintenance stay usable. The purge isn't about the content — it's about keeping the library a place you trust, where everything present has a reason to be.

ORANO (oranoai.com) prevents most purges by converting at save-time — but the monthly rule is worth keeping anyway, in any system, as the honesty check.
