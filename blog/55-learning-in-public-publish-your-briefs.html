Learning in public: publish your video briefs

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

If you've adopted the one-page brief habit — every saved video becomes a summary with timestamps, tasks, and cards — you're sitting on publishable content. Publishing briefs is learning in public at its cheapest, because the hard part (processing the video) is already done.

What a published brief does

For you: proof of expertise, in public, dated and cumulative. A hundred briefs on a topic is a portfolio no résumé matches. For the audience: someone else's conversion is a reading shortcut. The best briefs get shared precisely because they save the reader the watch.

The three formats that work

The brief itself — claim, takeaways, timestamps — posted where your industry reads. The meta-brief — "what three talks this month changed my mind about X," which combines briefs into an argument. The tool comparison — "how I'd process this video with three different tools," which is content and a review at once.

The quality bar

Publishing raises your own standard. A brief you know might be read makes you write better claims, verify timestamps, and honestly mark the entertainment. The public eye is a free QA layer — and the archive it produces is searchable proof of what you know.

ORANO (oranoai.com) generates the briefs from any saved video (iOS/Android/web); the publishing decision stays yours. Start with one brief a week — the format is the point, not the volume.
