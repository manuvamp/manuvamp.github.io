Save less, do more: the case for deleting your backlog

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Productivity culture treats your save pile as an asset. It is closer to a liability: 400 items of unpaid attention debt, compounding guilt, and search noise that buries the twenty things that ever mattered.

The audit

Open your saved content and ask three questions of every item: Did I act on this? Would I act on it this month if it were the only thing in the list? Can I name what it would change? Two noes means delete. In every audit I have run on my own library, the honest answer was delete for the strong majority.

Why deletion feels wrong and works anyway

Deletion triggers loss aversion — the same instinct that keeps junk drawers full. But the content is not actually lost: most of it is findable again in thirty seconds of search, and the part that isn't findable was never going to be used. What you gain is a library where everything is load-bearing.

Keep the reference, kill the fantasy

The distinction that makes deletion safe: keep reference (things you return to and reuse — with their key points extracted), delete fantasy (things saved for a version of you that does not exist). ORANO makes the split mechanical: actionable saves become projects with task lists, reference saves keep their takeaways, and everything is searchable forever — including what you deleted, if you saved it once.

Save less. Do more. Your library should be a workshop, not a warehouse.
