Spaced repetition for meetings

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every team has a retention problem it refuses to name: decisions made in meetings are forgotten within days, then re-litigated in new meetings. The standard fixes — better notes, recorded calls, searchable transcripts — all fail for the same reason. They store the information but never resurface it, and un-resurfaced information decays on schedule.

The study technique teams never borrowed

Students solved this decades ago with spaced repetition: review material at expanding intervals — a day, a week, a month — and retention compounds. The technique maps perfectly onto meeting output and almost no team uses it, because cards for a meeting must be generated by someone, and nobody volunteers.

What meeting flashcards look like

Not trivia. One card per durable decision: "Why did we drop the enterprise tier?" / "Q3 pricing test showed support cost ate the margin." One per constraint: "What can we not promise EU customers yet?" Six cards per meeting, reviewed in ninety seconds at the next meeting's open. Two months of that and the re-litigation meetings simply stop happening.

Generating the layer

The generation step is the chore, so automate it. Recording the meeting into ORANO produces the summary, the follow-up tasks, and the decision cards on a review schedule — the retention layer exists without anyone volunteering for homework.

The payoff

Meetings get shorter because fewer meetings exist to re-decide old decisions. The compounding is quiet but real: every recorded decision becomes a card, every card becomes shared memory.
