Meeting decisions into review cards

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

The most expensive meetings are the ones that re-decide settled questions. Not because anyone is careless, but because a decision that isn't resurfaced decays — and six weeks later, a new teammate or an old doubt reopens it, and five people spend an hour discovering what two of them already knew.

The card format

One decision, one card, one sentence answer. "Why did we drop the enterprise tier?" — "Q3 pricing test: support cost ate the margin." The card cites the recording's minute-mark, so any challenge ends in seconds. Cards that can't be written as one question and one sentence are the signal that the decision wasn't actually made.

Ninety seconds at the open

The review cadence is what makes it work: open the next meeting with the deck from prior meetings. Cards survive three reviews and graduate to assumed knowledge; reversed decisions retire and their reversal becomes a new card. The cost is roughly ninety seconds per meeting; the return is whole meetings that stop existing.

The automation

Writing cards by hand is the chore that kills the habit. ORANO generates them — save the recording, get the summary, the follow-up tasks, and a deck of decision cards on a review schedule — so the retention layer exists without depending on anyone's discipline.
