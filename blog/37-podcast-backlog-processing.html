Processing a podcast backlog in one weekend

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every podcast addict has a backlog that functions as a monument to good intentions. The backlog feels like homework, so it never gets processed, so it grows. Here is the two-day plan that actually worked for me, and the math that makes it realistic.

Saturday: triage by claim, not by episode

Read the episode titles and show notes only — no listening. For each episode write one candidate sentence: the claim it would prove or disprove. Half the backlog dies right here, because the claim was already covered by another episode or was never that interesting. You are left with the 20% that carries 80% of the value. Drop those into ORANO and let it produce summaries and timestamped key points overnight.

Sunday: the review pass

Read all the summaries in one sitting — this is the compounding step, because themes across episodes become visible when the summaries sit on one page. Mark follow-ups, delete redundant cards, and keep a dozen flashcards for the ideas you want at command. Total time: two to three hours.

Why a weekend works

The backlog dies of diffuse guilt, not difficulty. Two contained sessions with a hard output — a searchable page of summaries and a starter deck — replace years of ambient should. After the weekend, new episodes enter the same pipeline one at a time, and the monument never rebuilds.
