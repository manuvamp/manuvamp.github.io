The input diet: consume less, convert more

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every productivity problem gets diagnosed as "too much content". Wrong diagnosis. The volume was never the problem — a person who converts one video out of fifty into action is healthier than a person who saves nothing and learns nothing. The problem is conversion rate: of everything you take in, how much changes what you do on Monday?

The diet, in three rules

  • One in, one out. For every Reel or video you finish, one concrete thing must leave your head and land in your calendar, notes, or a task list. If nothing lands, the input was entertainment — fine, but call it that.
  • Saved is not consumed. The save button is a promise you make to your future self. Track how many of those promises you keep. For most people it is under five percent. Mine was two.
  • One weekly harvest. Once a week, walk through what you saved, keep only what still earns its place, and delete the rest without ceremony.

Where tooling earns its keep

The reason the diet fails is that conversion is expensive. Turning a 12-minute video into a decision means pausing, re-scrubbing, transcribing by memory, writing steps by hand. When every conversion costs twenty minutes, you convert nothing. That is the actual bottleneck — not your willpower.

This is what ORANO attacks directly. Share the video, and the understanding is done for you: a real summary, the key points worth keeping, and — when the content is actionable — an ordered task list with time and cost estimates. Run the diet for a month. The wins are not smaller — they are the same wins you were pretend-collecting for years, actually done.
