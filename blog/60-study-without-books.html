Study without books: video-first learning, done right

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Plenty of strong students never open the textbook — they study from lectures, walkthroughs, and recorded explainers. When it works, it's not because video is superior; it's because they've accidentally built a conversion pipeline. When it fails, it's because they stopped at watching.

The failure mode

Watching is exposure. Exposure produces recognition — "I've seen this" — which collapses under exam conditions, where production is required: answering from memory, applying to new problems. Re-watching doubles the exposure and adds nothing to production.

The conversion pipeline

Transcript pass (read in ten minutes, mark the five claims) → summary page (the claims compressed, with the lecturer's emphasis noted) → flashcards (the definitions and frameworks, scheduled for review at 3, 10, and 30 days). The video becomes a skimmable, testable, reviewable document. ORANO generates all three layers from any saved lecture automatically.

The schedule is the studying

Students assume the effort is in watching and summarizing. The evidence says otherwise: the scheduled retrieval — answering cards from memory at expanding intervals — is where grades are decided. Ten minutes of cards on day three outperforms an hour of re-watching the night before.
