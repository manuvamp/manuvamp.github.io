AI summaries are not cheating — they're triage

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Every time AI summarization comes up, someone objects that skipping to the summary "isn't real learning." Sometimes that's true. But the objection assumes a decision that most people haven't made: that every video deserved full attention in the first place. Summaries don't replace learning — they perform triage, deciding what deserves the real thing.

The emergency room model

A triage nurse doesn't treat anyone; they decide who gets treated and in what order. An AI summary of a saved video does exactly that. It reads the hour so you can spend your attention where it compounds — and for most content in most queues, the correct verdict is "skip," delivered with evidence rather than guilt.

Where the objection is right

If a summary is the *end* of the process, learning didn't happen — you've just automated skimming. The objection lands on tools that summarize and stop. The fix is structural, not moral: after a summary, the pipeline should force the next step — extract the actions, generate the review cards, schedule the resurfacing. Triage into treatment, not into a cleaner-looking archive.

The payoff

Full attention is scarce and should be spent like it. Triage the hour-long videos down to the twenty minutes that matter, convert those into notes and cards, and "real learning" stops being something you meant to do later.
