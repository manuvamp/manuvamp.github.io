Your shared drive is not a knowledge base

Founder notes · Manu Parasuraman

Teams handle recorded knowledge — webinars, all-hands recordings, customer calls, conference talks — by dumping files into a shared drive and declaring the problem solved. Then someone asks "didn't we cover this in a webinar last year?" and the search returns four versions of similar names and zero usable answers.

The three missing properties

A folder of recordings lacks the three things that make knowledge usable: a summary short enough to be read instead of watched, an owner responsible for keeping it current, and a decision trail connecting the content to what the team actually did. Without those, the drive is storage, not knowledge — an archive you hold out of fear rather than a tool you reach for.

The minimum viable knowledge base

The fix does not require new software. For each recording that matters: one page per recording with a five-line summary, the tasks it generated with owners and dates, and a link to the source with a timestamp. That page — not the recording — is what gets searched, linked, and cited in decisions. ORANO automates the page generation for video content, but a disciplined twenty minutes per recording with a text editor achieves the same structure.

The test

Ask a teammate to find what the company learned about pricing from last quarter's customer webinars. If the answer requires watching anything, you have a drive, not a knowledge base.
